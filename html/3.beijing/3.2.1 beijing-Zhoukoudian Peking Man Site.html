Zhoukoudian Peking Man Site Overview

A comprehensive guide to the Zhoukoudian Peking Man Site, an important archaeological site that provides insights into early human evolution.

The Zhoukoudian Peking Man Site is a UNESCO World Heritage site located in Fangshan District, Beijing. It is where the fossilized remains of Peking Man (Homo erectus) were discovered in the 1920s and 1930s.

The protection area of Zhoukoudian Site is 1,368 hectares. The site has yielded numerous important findings, including fossils of early humans, stone tools, and evidence of fire use. These discoveries have significantly contributed to our understanding of human evolution and prehistoric life in Asia.

Today, the site features a museum that displays the archaeological findings and provides information about the history and significance of the Peking Man discoveries. It is a must-visit destination for those interested in archaeology and human history.

Recommended Tour Route

The Zhoukoudian Peking Man Site is best explored by following a route that covers the main archaeological areas and the museum. Start at the Visitor Center to get an overview of the site's history and significance.

Then proceed to the Peking Man Cave, where the fossilized remains were discovered. This is the most important archaeological site in the area.

After exploring the cave, visit the Zhoukoudian Museum to see the exhibits and learn more about the discoveries. The museum features fossils, stone tools, and other artifacts found at the site.
